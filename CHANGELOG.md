# Changelog

and this project adheres to [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/spec/v2.0.0.html).

## [Unreleased]

### Added
- The watch opens with **Watch for**: **Motion and sound** or **Lost and Found**, above its camera and its settings. Motion and sound sounds an alert when something moves or makes a sound; Lost and Found also compares what the camera sees with a saved pet's photos, by colour and shape, while the watch runs. With Lost and Found chosen the watch shows that pet: its first saved photo, its name, its count of photos, **Change pet**, which opens **Choose a pet**, and the line on what it compares and its limits; with no pet saved, "No pet saved" and **Add a pet**, which opens the Lost & Found page. Choosing Motion and sound during a watch stops the comparison and Lost and Found starts it, with no restart of the camera, and a polite status says so. The choice is setup's own (`lostFoundChosen` in the onboarding store), so setup and the watch show the same one whatever the use, and the Lost & Found page's **Start Monitoring** opens the watch with Lost and Found and its pet. The pet is kept as `chosenPetId` in the Lost & Found store, so a reload keeps the choice and the pet; until one is chosen it is the newest saved pet, a thing saved as an object is the watch's only once chosen, and the Lost & Found page offers no **Start Monitoring** for a person an earlier version saved. In **Choose a pet** the arrow keys move the check and a press or Enter chooses; Escape and **Close** change nothing. The page's title reads "Motion and sound | SafeOS" or "Lost and Found | SafeOS". From 1024 px the pet's card heads the right column, and from 1440 px its other photos stand under it. While the watch compares, the Lost & Found panel under the alerts shows the pet's matches.
- A browser walk of Watch for in CI (`apps/guardian-ui/e2e/watch-for.spec.ts`, in the job "Web app Lost & Found browser test"), on the static build: a fresh device, no pet saved, a pet added on the Lost & Found page, Choose a pet, twelve pets, the saved pets' read failing or held, offline, the arrow keys, one row from 320 to 768 px, 200 per cent text, the widths' layouts, and axe on the watch at 390 and 1280 px in both looks (axe-core is installed for the run). The Pictures workflow captures the watch with Lost and Found chosen, with no pet saved and with a pet, at 390 and 1280 px in both looks.
- Setup offers Lost & Found beside the four uses. Choosing it saves the pet use, and the last key, **Open Lost and Found**, opens the Lost & Found page, where the pet's photos are added. The choice is saved with the use (`lostFoundChosen` in the onboarding store), so setup run again shows it chosen and ends on Lost & Found again. The question says to run setup again from the home page to change it, in place of naming Settings, which has no such control. The choices carry `aria-pressed`. Finishing setup opens the last key's page alone: the forwarding for a person who arrives with setup done stands down as the last step finishes.
- Error pages: a page that fails shows a message in place of a blank page. When a deploy has replaced the script files a tab or a kept page asks for, the page reloads once to fetch the newest build; any other error says the page stopped working, with a Reload key, a link home and the error's own words to report. A second missing file within five minutes shows that message instead of reloading again.
- A sample clip under `apps/guardian-ui/public/home/` (28 seconds, made from two CC0 1.0 recordings on Wikimedia Commons), with its captions and descriptions tracks and its record: both sources, the licence, the authors, the credit line and the command that made it.
- The Pictures workflow's job "Capture the home page pictures": it feeds Chromium's fake camera and microphone from the sample clip, lets the unchanged monitor watch it, and saves the home page's capture in three crops and both looks, the two install screenshots the manifest names, the shared-link card and `provenance.json`. They are committed under `apps/guardian-ui/public/` from the run's artifact, and a test fails a file that is missing or differs from that record.
- Every mail the API server sends (alerts, the settings test message, account verification, password reset) ends with a visible Unsubscribe link and carries `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click` (RFC 8058).
- `GET /api/mail/unsubscribe/:token` shows a page that asks and changes nothing; `POST` stops the mail at once (a blank `200` for a mailbox's one-click POST). No mail is sent to confirm an unsubscribe.
- A suppression list in the SafeOS database, read before every send. It stores only SHA-256 hashes of addresses and tokens.
- `src/cli/mail-suppress.ts` stops all mail, account mail included, to the addresses piped into it; with `--resubscribe` it lifts the unsubscribes of a person who asks for mail again (manual stops stay).
- `EmailSendError` lists the recipients that already got a mail when a send fails part-way.
- The API request log and the bundled Caddy access log print `[token]` in place of an unsubscribe token.
- `SAFEOS_API_PUBLIC_URL`: the https address the unsubscribe link points at (falls back to `https://SAFEOS_DOMAIN`).
- A browser test of Lost & Found in CI (`apps/guardian-ui/e2e/lost-found-edge.spec.ts`, job "Web app Lost & Found browser test"): it enrols a subject from a striped picture, shows Chromium's fake camera the same stripes and reads the Edge value of the saved match in the gallery. With `PLAYWRIGHT_BASE_URL` set, Playwright tests the site at that address and starts no development server.

### Fixed
- Repeats saved behind each other: a repeat counts onto the saved card in the order repeats are saved, and the card on screen keeps its newest reading; a repeat queued behind one that found its card cleared or acknowledged goes on that card's fresh card under the same clear boundary, so another tab's Clear all cancels it; this tab's Clear all drops the saves queued before it, so nothing returns to the emptied list; an acknowledgement of a repeat as it showed acknowledges the fresh card it went on, never the card another tab acknowledged; and each event's notifications have their own tag, kept when its alerts go on a new card and new for each event, so a new event does not replace an earlier one that is kept up. An acknowledgement of a saved card writes only the acknowledgement, so the saved count and reading stay, and while it waits to be saved the card shows with its saved reading and count, and a repeat redirected to a fresh card whose save failed goes with that save.
- Setup's last step for Lost & Found says where the comparison starts: Start Monitoring on the Lost & Found page, then Start Monitoring on Monitor to start the camera; the QuickStart says the same.

- The blog post on how SafeOS Guardian works names its detection model as it is: a pre-trained COCO-SSD on SSDLite MobileNet V2, its 4.5 million weights stored as 32-bit floats. It called the model quantized, and none of its weights are.
- Nap Mode watches. Start Nap Mode, after its check, applies the infant_sleep preset and hands the planned nap to Motion and sound, and the watch starts at once and sounds the alerts. It used to show a nap screen that ran no detector, whose camera preview was blank and whose "Movement Detected!" banner kept the check's result. The check now tests what the watch will use: the camera saved for the monitor, with or without a microphone, movement that lasts as long as the watch needs to alert, and the sound at the volume the nap's alerts will play at, saying before the start when quiet hours or mute will silence them, now or during the nap. During a nap every movement alert sounds that alarm at the effective volume as it plays, however small the movement, as the nap page says. The check samples motion every 200 ms, as the watch does, stops its camera and its sampling when the person goes back, and starts over on return, and its quiet-hours warning follows the clock and is checked again at Start. Once the planned minutes have passed the warning plays every 30 seconds at the effective volume, also while the camera is down, until Stop or End nap ends the nap. That returns to the Maximum Alert setting, as I'm Awake did, unless the person chose another setting during the nap. A reload or Back to the watch finds the nap again and waits for one tap on Resume nap, which turns the browser's sound on, so the nap's alarm and reminder can sound before the watch starts. A link, or a watch opened any other way, has no nap.
- The blog names the Frame.dev Team, with the Frame logo, as the author of its posts, in the byline, the structured data and the sign-off.
- The Test Alerts page's Preview Volume slider keeps its own value and plays the sound buttons under it at that volume, leaving the alert volume as set. It passed a hundredth of its value to the sound manager, whose volume runs from 0 to 100, and sprang back. Until the slider moves, it shows the effective volume, quiet hours included and as they begin or end, and the buttons play at it. The Emergency button says it always plays at full volume. Every test sound in Settings plays at exactly that volume, with no minimum under a warning or an alarm, and a one-shot sound played at a named volume keeps it when the alert volume moves to another level while it plays; a change to 0, mute or emergency mode still reaches it, and a loop follows the volume, so turning it down reaches a sound that plays on.
- Monitor alerts again while motion, sound or a pixel change goes on. Each detector stopped after its first alert until a reading fell under its line, so a person who kept moving in view got one alert and then none. While the readings stay over the line, the event now alerts again each time the longer of its severity's cooldown and the **Cooldown Period** has passed since its last alert: with the defaults (low 10 s, medium 30 s, high 60 s, critical 0 s, Cooldown Period 30 s) every 30 s at low, medium and critical and every 60 s at high. A rise in severity during the event waits only the new severity's cooldown, so a rise to critical alerts at once. Each repeat plays the sounds, vibrates and notifies again (the notification sets `renotify`, so it alerts when it replaces the earlier one), and updates the event's one card in place with the newest severity, reading and time and "Repeated N times"; escalation counts from the event's first alert. An acknowledged card stays as it is and the next repeat makes a new card. A custom sound set to loop for the trigger plays one copy while it loops, so repeats do not stack copies of it. The event ends once readings have stayed under the line for the detector's sustained window, or when monitoring stops or starts or the detector is turned off, so a scene that holds still for a moment is one event. The Cooldown Period's hint on the Monitor page and on Alert Escalation reads "Shortest time between repeated alerts for one event; a rise in severity alerts sooner". Motion and pixel readings during a continuing event also count as activity for the inactivity alert. `apps/guardian-ui/e2e/alerts-repeat.spec.ts` walks the monitor with a camera that keeps changing and with a tone on the microphone, run by CI's "Web app alert walk" job.
- A monitor event ends when its readings stay under the line for the sustained window, also when that window passes between two of the watch's samples: the next reading over the line starts a new event, which alerts on its own timing, in place of being held for the Cooldown Period as a repeat of the old one.
- A repeat alert reads its event's saved card in the same IndexedDB transaction as its write. When another tab acknowledged, dismissed or cleared the card, the repeat leaves it as that tab left it and goes on a fresh card, which the event's later repeats update. Repeats queued behind it go on that same fresh card, and a Clear all in another tab between the check and the fresh card's save cancels that save.
- History's Local Timeline, the dashboard, the alert panel and the alert menu list and show a card whose event repeated at its newest alert, with the event's start kept for escalation; the dashboard's alerts today counts it on the day of its newest alert. The dashboard's recent alerts and the alert menu sort by each card's newest alert before keeping five and ten.
- Lost & Found: a subject saved by the earlier version whose photos cannot be read again keeps how many photos its edges come from (`edgePhotos`), so each photo added later weighs the same as each one before it. The code's notes on edge pooling across grids name its limit.
- Lost & Found: a match is counted in one subject_profile transaction, so a fingerprint rebuilt or a photo added meanwhile is kept.
- Lost & Found: a cell a subject has left judges the light in the frame it is back in, so a subject that moves across a plain room is scored as the camera gives it in place of being darkened as a change of light.
- Settings > Sounds counts a play when an alert starts a custom sound; a repeat that finds the sound's loop playing counts none.
- A monitor notification keeps one tag a kind of alert, whatever its severity: a rise in severity during an event replaces the event's notification in place of standing beside it. Once an event has alerted at high or critical, each of its notifications stays up until it is dismissed, so a repeat at a lower severity does not replace it with one that closes by itself.
- The alert panel's screen-reader announcement names a card's repeat count ("Repeated 2 times."), so a repeat with the same reading and severity is read out.
- Lost & Found scores edges. Each region of a camera frame is read on the same 8 by 8 edge grid as the subject's photos, so a match's Edge value follows the picture. Regions were read on a 4 by 4 grid, which made Edge 0 for every match and 85 the highest score a match could reach. A subject's photos and the camera's regions are read on one fixed edge scale, on which a cell whose samples step 60 levels in red, green and blue to the next pixel right and down reads 100 (a step from black to white is 765). The same pixels give the same Edge value on both sides, and a camera's noise reads as weak as it is: a blank wall whose pixels alternate 8 levels reads 13 against black and white stripes' 100, and one level of noise reads 2. Edge grids of different sizes are compared on the coarser one, where a block of cells reads what the coarser grid reads, so weak edges stay weak. A region can score up to 15 points more than before, so the same saved Alert Threshold gives more alerts after the update, and the same record line saves more matches.
- Lost & Found's Color Sensitivity slider matches at every setting. Above 50 it lowers near matches (at 100 a score loses twice its distance from 100) and an exact match reads 100. At 100 it used to turn every score to 0, so nothing matched. Scores are whole numbers at every setting. A Color Sensitivity saved above 50 gives more matches after the update: at 75 a score of 80 read 40 and reads 70, and above 70 no match could reach the default record line of 50.
- Lost & Found's adaptive lighting moves a frame back to the brightness the scene has settled at when the light changes, by up to 64 levels, and both of the matcher's steps score the corrected frame. The settled brightness, and the spread of brightness within each cell, are kept for each cell of an 8 by 8 grid. A change of light is judged among the background cells. A cell more than 10 levels from where it settled that did not move with the frame's change of light (the same way, by half to twice as much) is foreground: it is left out of the judging and out of the settled brightness until it comes back, or until it has held for 300 frames and its brightness becomes the settled one. A subject that comes into view as the light changes is foreground too. With fewer than 8 background cells, the frame is scored as the camera gives it. A frame is corrected when seven in eight of the judged cells are brighter, or darker, than where they settled, or when half of them are and kept their texture, as a room does when a lamp comes on while its window stays as it was; it is moved by the median of those cells' differences. A subject in view does not move the settled brightness, so when it leaves as the light changes, the frame is moved by the change of light alone. Flat cells have no texture to judge, so a subject over a plain room is read as a change of light only when it covers seven in eight of the cells. The settled brightness follows the background slowly: a scene whose light holds is scored as the camera gives it, and a lasting change of light becomes the new settled brightness (half of it after about 35 frames). Frames from a camera started again, or from another camera, set the settled brightness afresh. The matcher reads the light into buffers it keeps, so a frame makes no new arrays for it. The correction used to drift toward the subject's own colour, which let any scene past the first step within a few frames, and regions were scored without it.
- A subject enrolled from several photos keeps each colour's share. Histogram buckets whose colours are closer than one bucket is wide are put together, and the merged histogram holds up to 32 buckets, those with the largest shares, so a small photo counts as much as a large one. Each photo counts the same in the histogram and in the averaged colour, size and edges, also when photos are added to a saved subject: the saved fingerprint counts as the photos it was made from. Before, buckets merged only on an identical colour, every share was divided by the number of photos, buckets past the first photo's count were dropped, and a saved subject counted as one photo when photos were added to it. A fingerprint now carries a version. A subject saved by an earlier version is rebuilt from its saved photos the first time it is watched on the Monitor page and when a photo is added to it, so each photo counts once and its edges are on the new scale. The rebuilt fingerprint replaces only the saved subject's fingerprint; a subject deleted meanwhile stays deleted, and photos, matches or a fingerprint saved meanwhile stay. A saved subject with no photos, or whose photos cannot be read, keeps its fingerprint, and the matcher reads the camera's edges for it the way that fingerprint was saved, each picture scaled to its own strongest cell; a photo added to such a subject gives it the new photo's edges.
- With motion priority on, Lost & Found scores a region when any of the four cells it covers moved. Motion in the last column or the last row of cells was never scored.
- The matcher's size tolerance (default 50; the app has no control for it) is the size difference, in points of the picture, at which the size score reaches 0. Nothing read the setting before. The default scores as before.
- The Motion and Audio Sensitivity set for a use on Settings > Detection tune the watch for that use. Set Baby Monitoring's Motion to 60 and the watch for a baby runs at 60, with a 40% motion threshold, where it ran at the preset's value before. Every Motion and Audio control on the Monitor page (Fine-Tune, the Sensitivity panel and Quick Settings) shows the value the watch runs at for its use and moves it where that value is kept: the use's value when one is set on Detection, the preset's otherwise. Apply Suggestions does the same. A preset change keeps the use's values, and Fine-Tune's note names them where it said every value lasted until the preset changed. Pixel Threshold, Analysis Interval and the processing mode come from the preset for every use, so a value for them in saved or imported settings for a use does not change the watch, and their sliders move the preset. Fine-Tune's Reset to the preset's defaults returns the preset's values and clears the Motion and Audio set for the use, as Reset to defaults on Detection does. A use with nothing set on Detection runs at the preset; a use saved by an earlier version with only Motion set keeps the preset's Audio.
- Setup can be finished without a camera. Until camera access is granted, the camera step shows **Continue without the camera** with the line "You can allow the camera later, on the Monitor page."; it records camera and microphone access as off, and setup goes on to the dashboard. After a failed request the step says what happened (the browser blocked the camera, the camera did not start, no camera was found, the chosen camera is unavailable, or the browser cannot use a camera on the page) and its key reads **Try again**. The scenario step shows **Continue** beside Back for a scenario already chosen. Before this, a refused or failed camera request left the step with no way on, and the dashboard sent the person back to setup.
- The built-in alert sounds play. The browser generates them as tones: one beep for a notification, two rising beeps for an alert, three beeps for a warning, two alternating tones for the alarm and a higher, faster pair for the emergency sound, each pair repeating while it loops. No sound file is fetched, so they play offline too. The five files the site served in their place held placeholder text, so every built-in sound failed to play.
- Several alerts at the top escalation level sound one emergency loop, and acknowledging them all silences it. An alarm or emergency loop that starts before the browser lets the page play sound, as before anyone clicks or taps it, waits and begins with the person's next click, tap or key press on the page, unless it was stopped meanwhile; a one-off sound that cannot play within a second is skipped. A sound stopped before it began makes no sound. A volume change or mute during a fade in takes effect at once, and a sound's fade ends when the sound does. While the browser holds the page's audio, a looping alarm queues no extra repeats, and it goes on at one repeat per cycle when the audio runs again.
- The emergency loop plays on without starting over when another alert arrives, when one of several alerts at the top level is acknowledged, and when the volume or mute changes; it stops when the last of them is acknowledged. When the sound set for the top level changes, the new sound takes over at once and the two do not sound together. A sound that is fading out goes silent at once when the volume or mute changes and does not come back on an unmute, and stopping it a second time does not lengthen its fade. A sound whose audio context has closed leaves the list of playing sounds, and the next sound plays on a new context.
- A queued action the server confirmed and the browser could not save keeps the server's answer and counts no failed try. The next sync applies that answer without a new request, and the five-try limit does not drop it; if the queue cannot be written either, the action stays as it was.
- A failed acknowledgement of a saved engine alert is retried with no clear stamp, so Clear all in any tab, which keeps engine alerts, does not cancel it. After Clear All Local Data or a sign-out the record is gone, and the retry writes nothing.
- Privacy distinguishes browser records from records kept on a configured server; clearing browser data or signing out does not delete server records.
- Browser alert history belongs to the browser: every page and tab shows the same saved alerts in any load order, whatever session is current, until someone signs out or clears local data. Alerts saved before this version stay until dismissed or cleared, whatever their date, outside the limits; later alerts are limited to 100 monitoring alerts and 500 overall, acknowledged ones leaving first, oldest first. No alert is removed for its age. Tabs still open from the previous version keep its 100-alert monitoring limit until they are closed or reloaded; the upgrade protects the records that exist when it runs. Acknowledgements and later writes keep an alert's media, creation time and protection; ids made in the browser carry a random part.
- A failed history read keeps the alerts on screen, and a failed save keeps the alert on screen and retries at the next read; an acknowledgement updates a pending save, and dismiss and clear drop it. An acknowledgement never puts back an alert that is no longer saved: the alert leaves that tab's list instead. Clear All Local Data, a sign-out or Clear all in any tab cancels every tab's pending saves: each clear writes a new clear generation in the database, and a retry writes only when it finds the generation its save holds or, for a save that holds none, the same marker in local storage; a browser that shows another tab's local storage write a moment late leaves a race in the instant of a clear. The upgrade keeps an unreadable creation date beside a usable one; if it cannot finish it changes nothing and the page says so. The alert engine alerts first and saves afterwards, and an acknowledgement made as the alert arrives is saved.
- A tab closes its database connection when a newer version opens in another tab, a tab held back by an older tab says so instead of waiting, and a database newer than the tab's code asks for a reload too. These notices ask for a reload and say what is paused until then. Whenever the database cannot be opened, a tab that holds a session for a configured server keeps it and makes no new one, also when a session request was already in flight; a tab that holds none, or whose session the server has ended, makes one. A session that ran out on this device just as a notice came is kept too, and the server's alerts pause in that tab until it is reloaded. Reload guidance remains readable in both light and dark looks.
- Profile and full exports omit unreadable or non-object monitoring and user notification settings, report separate omission counts, and leave stored rows unchanged. The export page reports both kinds of omissions and states the single-kind record limits. Export guidance distinguishes notification-setting merges from whole monitoring-setting replacements.
- Full server exports keep the newest limited rows and include total, included and truncated counts for streams, alerts and analysis results. Both profile and full exports redact provider credentials from monitoring settings while preserving readable settings' JSON-string format and stored values.
- Profile and full exports include owned monitoring profiles and personal active choices by ownership, with matching export guidance and documented record limits. Profiles with unreadable settings remain included with null settings and a count of omitted settings; exporting leaves stored rows unchanged.
- Full-export lists and their totals use the same database statement during monitoring writes. Profile and full downloads report omitted unreadable settings on the export page.
